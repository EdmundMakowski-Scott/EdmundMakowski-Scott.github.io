Honors program

Head curriculum developer, designing STEM classes for incarcerated students in Wisconsin.

Returning to teaching

During my time in the MSOE Honors Program, I have had the opportunity to return to my roots in teaching STEM to young and developing students. The primary point of the Honors Program is to let MSOE give back to the community around it. As such, it collects engineers who have a passion for social justice and gives them projects designed to do just that.

The project

I was picked to work on a particularly important project in which a group of engineers from diverse backgrounds is brought together to design a modular, engineering- and STEM-focused curriculum that we, or any other qualified individual, can then teach from. The whole project is designed specifically for underrepresented communities, specifically people currently serving time in Wisconsin prison institutions.

This means that in addition to creating an engineering curriculum from scratch and teaching it, we must also work with the Wisconsin Department of Corrections (DOC), the Department of Justice (DOJ), and the individual institution where we plan to implement the course to work out the specific requirements of the facility.

What it has given me

Overall, this project was not at all what I expected to be working on while pursuing an education in engineering, but it has benefited me all the same. It has allowed me to go back through my courses and understand them through a new and more detailed lens, detailed enough to teach from start to finish. It also lets me work, in a small way, toward prison and education reform, which was a great passion of mine throughout high school, where I pursued an education in psychology and education at Concordia University.

I would like to emphasize that my work in this area has been minimal in reach. Hopefully it has laid the groundwork for similar work in the future, which in my eyes is at the very least a small step in the right direction.
